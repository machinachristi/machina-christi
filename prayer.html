How to read the pronunciation

Thai is tonal: the same syllable at a different pitch is a different word. The mark over each vowel gives its tone.

naa
no mark — mid, level and even
nàa
low — flat and low
nâa
falling — starts high, then drops
náa
high — pitched up
nǎa
rising — dips, then climbs, like a question

Doubled vowels (aa, ii, uu, ee, oo) are held longer. ph, th, kh are p, t, k with a puff of air, as in Phuket or Thai — never English “f” or “th”. bp and dt are a crisp p and t with no puff at all. ae as in “cat”, aw as in “law”, oe as in “her”, and ue like “oo” said with the lips spread.
