The name

Machina Christi means “the machine of Christ”. On his way to Rome and martyrdom, Ignatius wrote to the Christians of Ephesus that they were stones for God’s temple, lifted into place by the Cross, Christ’s builder’s crane. His Greek word, mēchanē, is the word behind the Latin machina. The Holy Spirit is the rope, faith the windlass that winds it, and love the road up.

Ignatius used the machine of his day. Ours is the one in your hand:

  • The Cross is the tower. It was raised to reach down, where Babel was built to climb up: “I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself” (John 12:32).
  • The Spirit is the signal, unseen like the wind, and heard in every tongue (John 3:8; Acts 2:6).
  • Faith is the receiver: “Faith then cometh by hearing” (Romans 10:17).
  • Hope is the city coming down from God, the new Jerusalem (Apocalypse 21:2).
  • Love is the network that hands it on: the whole body “fitly joined together, by what every joint supplieth” (Ephesians 4:16).

And this website? One stretch of the wall: the part in front of my own house, as each family in Nehemiah’s Jerusalem rebuilt the part by its own door (Nehemiah 3:28). Pope Leo XIV puts the choice about technology the same way: Babel or Jerusalem (Magnifica Humanitas, ¶9 and ¶13).
