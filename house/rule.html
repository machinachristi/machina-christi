The rule of the machine

Seven promises for how this machine is built and run, so that it serves the people who use it and never uses them.

Every design choice carries a picture of what a person is, Pope Leo XIV reminds the people who build technology (Magnifica Humanitas ¶111). These are this site’s choices, written down so they can be kept and checked.

  1. People, never parts

    Every visitor is a face, not a number to grow. No accounts, no profiles, no streaks, no scores.

    Magnifica Humanitas ¶114, ¶170

  2. Lift, don’t hold

    Pages end. No feeds, no endless scroll, nothing built to keep you here. The best visit may be a short one that sends you off to pray.

    The attention economy: Magnifica Humanitas ¶170

  3. Signal, not noise

    Quiet by default: no pop-ups, no alerts to pull you back, room for silence. The only bell is the Angelus.

    Rhythms of silence: Magnifica Humanitas ¶146, ¶190

  4. Every tongue, truly

    Thai and English side by side, each checked by people who speak it. A machine’s translation is never passed off as the Church’s words.

    Acts 2:6; the “Babel syndrome”: Magnifica Humanitas ¶10

  5. Hand it on faithfully

    The prayers are the Church’s own words, always with their source. AI helps build the house, but never writes a prayer and never pretends to be a person, and a machine voice always says that it is one.

    Magnifica Humanitas ¶99–100, ¶233

  6. Show the wiring

    How it is built is open to see in the Workshop. What is counted, and why, is said plainly, and a visitor who asks not to be counted is not counted.

    Magnifica Humanitas ¶164, ¶178

  7. Sabbath, and count the cost

    The builders rest on Sunday. The machine’s cost in money, energy and water is kept small and known, and every tool is judged by its fruit, not its traffic.

    Luke 14:28; Matthew 7:16; Magnifica Humanitas ¶101, ¶169

The test for anything new

Does it help someone love God or a neighbour, or show how the house is built? If it does, it gets a room.
